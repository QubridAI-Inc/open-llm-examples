
**Step‑by‑step plan for answering “What was the average revenue growth of top SaaS companies in 2023?”**

| Step | What to do | Why it matters | Tool(s) to use |
|------|------------|----------------|----------------|
| 1️⃣ | **Define “top SaaS companies.”**  <br>‑ Decide on a clear, objective criterion (e.g., market‑cap rank, revenue rank, or inclusion in a recognized list such as the *Forbes Cloud 100* or *Gartner Magic Quadrant*).  <br>‑ Write down the exact list of companies you will include (e.g., the 10 largest SaaS firms by 2023 revenue). | A well‑defined set prevents ambiguity and ensures you are averaging the same cohort every time. | No external tool needed; just a written definition. |
| 2️⃣ | **Gather each company’s 2023 revenue‑growth figure.**  <br>‑ Search for the most recent, reliable source that reports the *year‑over‑year* revenue growth for each company (e.g., 2023 fiscal‑year filings, earnings releases, analyst reports, or reputable market‑research publications).  <br>‑ Record the growth percentage (or growth factor) for every company on your list. | You need accurate, source‑backed numbers; “average” is only as trustworthy as the underlying data. | **Web search** – to locate earnings releases, SEC filings, or market‑research summaries that disclose 2023 growth rates. |
| 3️⃣ | **Verify the data source.**  <br>‑ Check that the source is reputable (e.g., company’s 10‑K/20‑F filing, earnings call transcript, or a well‑known analyst report).  <br>‑ Note the date of the source to confirm it covers the *full* 2023 fiscal year. | Guarantees the numbers are not outdated, estimated, or biased. | **Web search** (to locate primary sources) and optionally **Database query** if you have an internal database of financial statements. |
| 4️⃣ | **Extract the growth numbers into a structured format.**  <br>‑ Create a simple table (e.g., Company | 2023 Revenue Growth %) with one row per company. | A structured table makes the next calculation straightforward and transparent. | **Calculator** (or a spreadsheet) – just to organize the numbers; no calculation yet. |
| 5️⃣ | **Calculate the arithmetic mean of the growth rates.**  <br>‑ Add all the growth percentages together.  <br>‑ Divide the sum by the number of companies in your list. | The arithmetic mean is the standard way to compute an “average” when the data are expressed as percentages. | **Calculator** – to perform the addition and division accurately. |
| 6️⃣ | **Document the calculation steps.**  <br>‑ Show the sum of the growth rates.  <br>‑ Show the division by the count.  <br>‑ State the final average (e.g., “The average revenue growth of the selected 10 SaaS firms in 2023 was X %”). | Transparency lets anyone verify the result and understand how it was derived. | **Calculator** (to display the intermediate sums and quotient). |
| 7️⃣ | **State any assumptions or limitations.**  <br>‑ Mention the list size, the fiscal‑year definition used (calendar vs. fiscal), and any exclusions (e.g., companies that did not report 2023 data).  <br>‑ Note that the average is a simple mean; other aggregations (weighted by revenue) could yield a different figure. | Readers need to know the context and potential biases of the result. | No tool required; just a written disclaimer. |
| 8️⃣ | **Provide the final answer.**  <br>‑ Present the computed average growth rate, citing the sources used for each company’s growth figure. | Gives the user the answer they asked for, backed by a clear methodology. | Summarize the result in plain language. |
